# Wholesale Iranian Granite Blocks for Export to Turkey and Iraq

Turkey and Iraq are two of Iran's most important regional stone export markets. Proximity, shared borders, and established land transport routes make these countries natural destinations for Iranian granite blocks. Whether you are a stone factory in Turkey looking for raw blocks or a construction supplier in Iraq needing granite for projects, understanding the sourcing and logistics process helps you get the best stone at the right price. This guide covers quarry sources, block grades, pricing, and the most efficient shipping routes for wholesale granite exports to both countries.

## Iranian Granite Quarry Sources

Iran's granite production is concentrated in a few key regions, each producing distinct colors and grades. Understanding these sources is the first step in sourcing the right granite for your market.

**Natanz, Isfahan Province** is Iran's premier granite quarrying region. The Natanz quarries are best known for red granite — a deep, uniform red that is highly sought after for monuments, cladding, and decorative construction. Natanz also produces pink granite and some gray varieties. The quarries around Natanz have been operating for decades and have established processing infrastructure nearby. Red granite from Natanz is the most widely exported Iranian granite, with consistent quality and color uniformity that makes it popular in both Turkish and Iraqi markets.

**Urmia, West Azerbaijan Province** is a secondary granite source with a significant logistical advantage: proximity to the Turkish border. Urmia granite quarries produce gray and pink varieties. For buyers in Turkey, sourcing from Urmia can dramatically reduce transport time and cost compared to shipping from Natanz or Bandar Abbas. The Urmia region also produces building stone and some marble, making it a versatile sourcing destination for regional buyers.

**Other regions** — including Yazd and South Khorasan — produce smaller quantities of granite, including black granite. Black granite from Iran is less common than red or gray but is available for specialty applications such as monument bases and interior flooring.

## Granite Block Grades and Specifications

Iranian granite blocks are classified into grades based on color uniformity, structural integrity, and block size. Understanding these grades helps you specify the right material for your processing needs.

**Super grade** blocks have uniform color, no visible cracks or veins, and are free of inclusions. These blocks are suitable for high-end cladding, polished slabs, and monument production. Super grade red granite from Natanz commands the highest prices.

**First grade** blocks have minor color variation or small geological features but are structurally sound. These are the workhorse grade for most processing factories — suitable for tiles, slabs, and standard construction applications.

**Commercial grade** blocks may have more color variation, minor cracks, or smaller block sizes. These are priced lower and are often used for cut-to-size projects, pavers, or where appearance is less critical.

Block dimensions are another key specification. Standard quarry blocks range from 2 to 3 cubic meters (approximately 240×120×120 cm), but larger blocks up to 5–6 cubic meters are available on request. For gang saw processing, larger blocks are more efficient as they produce more slabs per cutting cycle. For block cutters or smaller processing equipment, standard blocks are sufficient.

## FOB and DAP Pricing for Regional Markets

Pricing for Iranian granite blocks depends on the granite color, grade, block size, and the delivery term. For regional buyers in Turkey and Iraq, both sea and land routes are viable, which affects the pricing structure.

**FOB Bandar Abbas** pricing for Iranian granite blocks typically ranges from $150 to $300 per ton. Red granite from Natanz is at the mid-to-upper range ($200–$300 per ton for super and first grade), while gray granite is more affordable ($150–$220 per ton). Pink granite falls in between. Block size also affects price — larger blocks command a premium because they yield more usable slabs.

**DAP border pricing** is more relevant for land exports. DAP (Delivered at Place) pricing includes the cost of the blocks plus trucking to the agreed border crossing. For Turkey, DAP Bazargan border pricing from Urmia quarries is typically $180–$340 per ton, depending on grade. The trucking cost from Urmia to Bazargan is relatively modest (approximately 200 km), making this route very cost-competitive.

For Iraq, DAP Khosravi or DAP Mehran border pricing depends on the origin quarry. Granite from Natanz trucked to the Iraq border involves a longer haul (over 1,000 km), so the trucking component is significant. Buyers should compare DAP border pricing against sea freight to Umm Qasr to determine the most economical route.

## Shipping Routes: Land vs. Sea

The choice between land and sea transport is one of the most important decisions for granite exports to Turkey and Iraq. Each route has distinct advantages.

### Land Route to Turkey via Bazargan

For destinations in eastern, central, and even western Turkey, land transport via the Bazargan border crossing is often the fastest and most cost-effective option. The route runs from Urmia (or Natanz) to the Bazargan border post in West Azerbaijan, then into Turkey's eastern provinces. From there, trucks continue to destination cities via Turkey's highway network.

A truckload of granite blocks (typically 25–27 tons) can reach Istanbul from the Bazargan border in 3–4 days, including border clearance. This is significantly faster than sea freight, which requires trucking to Bandar Abbas, container loading, ocean transit to a Turkish port (Mersin or Istanbul), and then inland transport to the final destination.

### Land Route to Iraq

For Iraq, land routes via the Khosravi (Khanaqin) or Mehran border crossings are the standard for granite exports. These crossings connect Iran to Iraq's central and southern provinces. The Mehran border is closer to Baghdad and southern Iraq, while Khosravi provides access to the Diyala governorate and Baghdad region.

Trucking time from Natanz to the Iraq border is 2–3 days, plus 1–2 days for border clearance. From the border, trucks continue to Baghdad, Basra, or Erbil depending on the destination. For northern Iraq (Kurdistan Region), the Parviz Khan border crossing is also used.

### Sea Route via Bandar Abbas

Sea freight via Bandar Abbas is an alternative for both markets, particularly for large-volume shipments. For Turkey, containers are shipped to Mersin or Istanbul ports. For Iraq, the destination is Umm Qasr port. Sea freight is more economical for large volumes (multiple containers) but involves longer transit times and requires both export and import port handling.

## Export and Transit Documentation

Land exports require a slightly different documentation set than sea exports. In addition to the standard export documents — commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and export declaration — land shipments require a **CMR consignment note**. The CMR is the international road transport document that serves as the contract of carriage between the sender and the carrier.

For transit shipments (where the truck crosses through Iran to reach a third country), transit documents may be required. These allow the truck to pass through Iranian territory without being treated as an import. Your freight forwarder or customs broker arranges these documents.

The certificate of origin is particularly important for both Turkey and Iraq. Turkey may apply preferential tariffs based on the origin certificate, and Iraqi customs requires it for clearance. The certificate must be issued by the Iran Chamber of Commerce and may need legalization by the Turkish or Iraqi embassy in Iran, depending on current requirements.

## Border Clearance Procedures

Border clearance is the most critical step in land exports. Unlike sea freight, where clearance happens at a port with established procedures, land exports require coordination between customs authorities on both sides of the border.

At the Iranian side (Bazargan, Khosravi, or Mehran), the truck presents the export declaration, CMR, and supporting documents to Iranian border customs. The cargo is verified, and exit clearance is granted. On the destination side (Turkey or Iraq), the truck presents the same documents (plus the certificate of origin) to the importing country's customs. Import duties and taxes are assessed based on the destination country's tariff schedule.

Having customs brokers on both sides of the border is essential. The Iranian customs broker handles exit procedures, while a Turkish or Iraqi broker handles entry. Your freight forwarder typically coordinates both, but you should confirm this is included in their service.

## How to Export Iranian Granite Blocks to Turkey and Iraq

### 1. Identify the granite type and color

Iran produces red granite (Natanz), pink granite, gray granite, and black granite. Identify the color and grade your Turkish or Iraqi market demands. Red granite is popular for monuments and decorative cladding, while gray granite is widely used for construction and infrastructure projects. Pink granite serves niche decorative markets.

### 2. Source from the right quarry

Natanz in Isfahan province is the primary granite quarrying region. Urmia in West Azerbaijan also has granite quarries with logistical advantages for land export to Turkey. For Turkey-bound shipments, Urmia offers significant transport savings. For Iraq, Natanz is the primary source, with trucking to the southern or eastern borders.

### 3. Request wholesale FOB or DAP pricing

For Turkey and Iraq, both sea (via Bandar Abbas) and land routes are viable. FOB Bandar Abbas is standard; for land routes, ask for DAP border pricing (e.g., DAP Bazargan border for Turkey). Compare both options to determine the most cost-effective delivered price to your destination.

### 4. Choose the shipping route

For Turkey, land transport via the Bazargan border is often faster and cheaper than sea. For Iraq, land routes via the Khosravi or Mehran borders are common. Sea routes via Bandar Abbas to Umm Qasr are also used. Consider transit time, cost, and border crossing conditions when making your choice.

### 5. Prepare export and transit documents

For land exports, you need a CMR consignment note in addition to the standard export documents. Transit documents may be required for cross-border transport through Iran. Ensure the certificate of origin is legalized if required by the destination country. All document values and descriptions must be consistent.

### 6. Arrange border clearance

Land exports require clearance at the border crossing. Your supplier or freight forwarder coordinates with customs brokers on both sides of the border for smooth transit. Confirm that the freight forwarder has brokers at both the Iranian exit point and the destination entry point to avoid delays at the border.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What types of Iranian granite are available for export to Turkey and Iraq?

Iran produces red granite (primarily from Natanz), pink granite, gray granite, and black granite. Red and gray granite are the most commonly exported varieties to Turkey and Iraq for construction and monument use. Black granite is available in smaller quantities for specialty applications.

### Is land transport cheaper than sea freight for granite exports to Turkey?

For Turkey, land transport via the Bazargan border is often faster and more cost-effective than sea freight via Bandar Abbas, especially for destinations in eastern or central Turkey. For western Turkey, sea freight may be competitive. Compare DAP border pricing against FOB plus sea freight plus inland transport.

### What is the FOB price of Iranian granite blocks for wholesale export?

FOB Bandar Abbas prices for Iranian granite blocks typically range from $150 to $300 per ton depending on color, grade, and block size. Red granite from Natanz is at the mid-to-upper range. Gray granite is the most affordable option for large-volume wholesale purchases.

You need a commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, export declaration, and a CMR consignment note for land transport. Transit documents may be required depending on the border crossing used. The certificate of origin should be issued by the Iran Chamber of Commerce.

### Which border crossings are used for stone exports from Iran to Turkey and Iraq?

For Turkey, the Bazargan border (West Azerbaijan) is the main crossing. For Iraq, the Khosravi and Mehran borders are commonly used. For the Kurdistan Region of Iraq, the Parviz Khan border is also an option. Your freight forwarder selects the best route based on your destination.
