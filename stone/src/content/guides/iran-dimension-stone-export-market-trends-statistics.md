# Iran Dimension Stone Export Market Trends and Statistics (2024–2026)

Iran's dimension stone industry is a significant player in the global stone trade. With vast geological reserves and thousands of active quarries, Iran consistently ranks among the world's top stone-producing nations. Understanding the market trends and export statistics for 2024–2026 helps buyers and investors make informed sourcing decisions. This guide provides a data-driven overview of Iran's dimension stone production, export destinations, price trends, competitive positioning, and the outlook for the coming years. Whether you are a stone importer assessing supply risk, a project developer evaluating sourcing options, or an investor analyzing the market, this analysis gives you the context you need to navigate the Iranian stone trade with confidence.

## Iran's Dimension Stone Production Base

Iran's geological diversity gives it one of the broadest dimension stone resource bases in the world. The country has over 12,000 registered quarries producing travertine, marble, granite, onyx, limestone, and crystal stone. The provinces of East Azerbaijan (Azarshahr travertine), Fars (Dehbid marble), Kermanshah (Harsin marble and Pietra Gray), Isfahan (Lashotor marble and Natanz granite), and West Azerbaijan (Takab travertine) are the primary production hubs.

Annual production capacity is estimated at over 15 million tons of dimension stone across all types. Travertine accounts for the largest share of production by volume, followed by marble, granite, and onyx. The production base has grown steadily over the past decade as new quarries have been opened and existing ones expanded. However, production is not evenly distributed — travertine and marble quarries are concentrated in specific regions, and the quality of output varies significantly between quarries even within the same stone type.

A notable characteristic of Iran's stone industry is the predominance of block exports over processed products. While Turkey and Italy have developed extensive slab and tile processing infrastructure, Iran exports a larger proportion of raw blocks, which are processed at destination. This is changing as Iranian processors invest in gangsaws and polishing lines, but blocks remain the dominant export format, particularly to China.

## Export Volumes and Key Destination Markets

Iran's dimension stone exports are concentrated in a handful of key destination markets. China is by far the largest importer of Iranian stone, accounting for the majority of block exports. Chinese buyers import Iranian travertine and marble blocks for processing into slabs and tiles in their domestic cutting facilities. The volume of stone flowing from Bandar Abbas to Chinese ports such as Xiamen and Qingdao is the single largest trade lane for Iranian dimension stone.

Iraq is the second-largest destination, driven by proximity and reconstruction demand. Iraqi buyers import both blocks and processed stone for construction projects across the country. Turkey is both a competitor and a customer — Turkish processors import Iranian blocks, process them, and re-export finished products under Turkish branding. The UAE, particularly Jebel Ali, serves as both a destination market and a transshipment hub for stone moving to other Gulf and African markets.

India is a growing destination, with buyers in Gujarat and Rajasthan importing Iranian travertine and marble blocks for processing. European markets remain smaller in volume but are growing for specialty stones — particularly Dehbid white marble, onyx, and Pietra Gray — where Iranian stone offers aesthetic and price advantages over European alternatives.

## Price Trends for 2024–2025

FOB prices for Iranian dimension stone have shown relative stability through 2024, with moderate upward pressure on premium varieties. Silver and beige travertine, the highest-volume export categories, have remained in the $150 to $230 per ton FOB Bandar Abbas range for commercial and first grades. Super-grade travertine has seen modest price increases, driven by Chinese demand for high-quality blocks suitable for large-format slab production.

Marble prices have been more dynamic. Pietra Gray has remained stable at $200 to $280 per ton, reflecting consistent demand from Middle Eastern markets. Dehbid white marble has experienced upward price pressure, with super-grade blocks reaching $450 to $500 per ton, driven by limited quarry output and strong demand from European and Chinese buyers. The premium for large, uniform Dehbid blocks has widened as architects and designers increasingly specify this stone for high-end projects.

Currency dynamics have been a significant factor in 2024–2025 pricing. The Iranian rial's exchange rate against the US dollar directly affects the USD-denominated FOB prices that exporters can offer. Periods of rial depreciation have given Iranian exporters room to offer more competitive USD pricing, while rial appreciation has put upward pressure on quotes. Buyers should monitor exchange rate trends when timing large purchases.

## How to Analyze Iran Dimension Stone Export Market Trends

Analyzing market trends requires a structured approach to data collection and interpretation. Below is a step-by-step methodology for assessing the Iranian dimension stone export market.

### 1. Review production data

Start with Iran's annual dimension stone production figures by type — travertine, marble, granite, and onyx — to understand the supply base and growth trajectory. Production data is published by Iran's Ministry of Industry, Mine and Trade and can be cross-referenced with ITC and UN Comtrade trade flow data. Look at multi-year trends rather than single-year snapshots, as annual production can fluctuate due to quarry openings, closures, and seasonal factors.

### 2. Analyze export volumes by destination

Examine export data by country to identify which markets are growing and which are contracting. China, Iraq, Turkey, and the UAE are the largest destinations. UN Comtrade provides bilateral trade data under HS codes 2515 (marble, travertine, alabaster) and 2516 (granite). Track year-over-year changes in export volume by destination to identify emerging markets and declining ones. For example, if exports to Vietnam or Indonesia are growing year over year, these may represent emerging opportunities for suppliers.

### 3. Track price trends

Monitor FOB price movements for key stone types over time. Price trends reveal supply-demand dynamics and help you time your purchases. FOB prices are not published in a single centralized database, but they can be triangulated from proforma invoices, trade data unit values, and supplier quotes. Track prices for the specific stone types and grades you buy — aggregate market prices mask significant variation between premium and commercial grades. For current FOB price ranges, see our guide on [Iranian travertine and marble FOB prices at Bandar Abbas](/guides/iranian-travertine-marble-fob-price-bandar-abbas/).

### 4. Assess competitive positioning

Compare Iran's export volumes and prices against competitors like Turkey, Italy, and India to understand relative market position and cost advantages. Turkey is the most direct competitor — both countries produce travertine and marble, and both export to overlapping markets. Iran's primary competitive advantage is cost: lower quarrying and labor costs translate into FOB prices that are typically 20 to 40 percent below Turkish equivalents for comparable stone types. Turkey's advantage is in processing infrastructure, branding, and market access. For a detailed comparison, see our guide on [Iran's decorative stone export advantages over Italy and Turkey](/guides/iran-decorative-stone-export-advantages-italy-turkey/).

### 5. Identify regulatory and market shifts

Track changes in import regulations in key markets (e.g., CE marking in the EU, sanctions compliance) that could affect export flows. Regulatory changes can open or close markets with little warning. For example, changes in EU radioactivity testing requirements or the introduction of new documentation standards in Gulf markets can affect which shipments clear customs and which are held. Stay informed through trade associations, customs broker updates, and supplier communications. For documentation requirements that may be affected by regulatory shifts, see our guide on [documents required for exporting marble from Iran](/guides/documents-exporting-marble-iran-certificate-origin/).

### 6. Forecast demand for 2026

Combine production capacity, export trends, and construction industry forecasts in key markets to project demand for Iranian stone in 2026. The outlook for 2026 is cautiously positive. Construction demand in China, the Gulf, and India is expected to remain strong, supporting continued import of Iranian blocks. European demand for specialty stones (Dehbid white marble, onyx, Pietra Gray) is likely to grow as architects and designers seek alternatives to increasingly expensive Italian stone. The main downside risks are currency volatility, shipping cost fluctuations, and potential regulatory changes in key destination markets.

## Competitive Landscape: Iran vs. Turkey vs. Italy

Iran, Turkey, and Italy are the three largest dimension stone-producing countries in the region, and each occupies a distinct competitive position. Italy is the premium brand leader — Italian marble (Carrara, Calacatta) commands the highest prices globally, supported by centuries of brand recognition and advanced processing technology. However, Italian quarrying costs are high, and reserves are finite, creating opportunities for alternative sources.

Turkey is the volume leader in processed stone exports, with a well-developed slab and tile processing industry and strong branding in international markets. Turkish travertine (particularly Denizli and Afyon) is a direct competitor to Iranian travertine. Turkey's advantages are processing infrastructure, proximity to European markets, and established brand recognition. However, Turkish FOB prices are generally higher than Iranian prices for comparable stone.

Iran competes primarily on cost and stone variety. With lower quarrying costs, abundant reserves, and unique stone types (Dehbid white marble, Pietra Gray, red travertine, onyx), Iran offers buyers a compelling value proposition — particularly for block imports that are processed at destination. The gap between Iran and Turkey in processing infrastructure is narrowing as Iranian processors invest in modern equipment, which could shift more value-added exports to Iran over time.

## Outlook for 2025–2026

The outlook for Iran's dimension stone exports in 2025–2026 is one of steady growth with headwinds. On the positive side, construction demand in Asia and the Middle East remains robust, the rial's exchange rate continues to make Iranian stone cost-competitive, and growing awareness of unique Iranian stone varieties is expanding the buyer base. On the challenging side, shipping costs from Bandar Abbas can be volatile, regulatory complexity in some destination markets creates friction, and competition from Turkey and emerging producers like Vietnam is intensifying.

For buyers, the strategic implication is that 2025–2026 is a favorable window for sourcing Iranian stone. Cost advantages are likely to persist, unique stone varieties remain available, and the supplier base is maturing with improved quality control and documentation capabilities. Building supplier relationships now positions buyers to benefit from this market over the medium term.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much dimension stone does Iran export annually?

Iran exports several million tons of dimension stone annually, making it one of the top stone-exporting nations. Exact figures vary by year and source, but travertine and marble dominate export volumes.

### Which countries import the most Iranian stone?

China is the largest importer of Iranian stone blocks, followed by Iraq, Turkey, the UAE, and India. European markets are growing for finished slabs and specialty marble.

### Is Iran's stone export market growing in 2025–2026?

The market is generally expanding, driven by construction demand in Asia and the Middle East. However, growth is influenced by currency fluctuations, shipping costs, and regulatory changes in destination markets.

### How does Iran compare to Turkey in dimension stone exports?

Both are major exporters, but Iran offers cost advantages due to lower quarrying costs and abundant reserves. Turkey has stronger branding and processing infrastructure. Iran competes primarily on price and stone variety.

### What stone types are driving Iran's export growth?

Travertine remains the flagship export, but Pietra Gray marble, white Dehbid marble, and onyx are seeing increased demand from architects and designers globally.

The ITC (International Trade Centre) at intracen.org, UN Comtrade at comtradeplus.un.org, and Iran's own customs and mining organizations publish trade data for dimension stone.
