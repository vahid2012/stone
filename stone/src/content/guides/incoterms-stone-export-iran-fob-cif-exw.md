# Incoterms for Stone Export from Iran: FOB, CIF and EXW Explained

Incoterms define who is responsible for what in an international stone transaction — who pays for transport, who handles customs, when risk transfers, and where costs begin and end. For Iranian stone exports, three Incoterms are most common: EXW (Ex Works), FOB (Free on Board), and CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight). Understanding the differences is essential for comparing quotes accurately and avoiding unexpected costs. This guide explains each Incoterm in the specific context of Iranian stone shipments, covering the inland journey from quarries in Isfahan, Azarshahr, or Kerman to the export port at Bandar Abbas, the ocean leg to your destination, and the critical points where risk and cost responsibility shift between supplier and buyer.

## Why Incoterms Matter for Iranian Stone Exports

Iran's stone export supply chain has a particular structure that makes Incoterm selection especially important. Quarries are located inland — often 500 to 800 kilometers from the port of Bandar Abbas — and the inland trucking leg involves Iran's domestic transport network, export clearance at Iranian customs, and loading at a port that operates under specific procedures. Unlike exports from countries with multiple coastal ports near quarry regions, almost all Iranian stone exits through a single major port, which means the inland transport cost is a significant component of the total FOB price.

The Incoterm you choose determines whether the supplier or the buyer bears the cost and risk of each segment: quarry loading, inland trucking, port handling, export customs, ocean freight, insurance, and destination port charges. A misunderstanding about which party covers, for example, the terminal handling charges at Bandar Abbas or the export clearance fee can add hundreds of dollars per container in unexpected costs. ## EXW (Ex Works): Maximum Buyer Responsibility

Under EXW, the supplier's only obligation is to make the stone available at the quarry or processing facility. The buyer is responsible for everything else: loading blocks onto trucks, inland transport to Bandar Abbas, export clearance through Iranian customs, port handling, ocean freight, insurance, and all destination charges. The supplier provides the commercial invoice and packing list but is not obligated to assist with export documentation or logistics.

EXW gives the buyer maximum control over every link in the chain and results in the lowest quoted unit price, since the supplier is not adding any logistics margin. However, it is only practical for buyers who have a reliable logistics partner physically present in Iran — a freight forwarder or agent who can arrange trucking, handle customs clearance, and manage port loading. For most international buyers, especially first-time importers, EXW is not recommended because the complexity of Iran's export procedures and the risk of inland transport delays can easily outweigh the price savings. A blocked truck at an inland customs checkpoint or a missing export permit can strand cargo for weeks.

## FOB (Free on Board): The Standard for Iranian Stone

FOB Bandar Abbas is the most widely used Incoterm for Iranian stone exports, and for good reason. Under FOB, the supplier handles the entire inland chain: loading blocks at the quarry, trucking to Bandar Abbas, export clearance, port handling, and loading the stone onto the vessel. Once the goods cross the ship's rail — or under the newer Incoterms 2020 rules, once the goods are loaded on board the vessel — risk and cost responsibility transfer to the buyer. From that point, the buyer is responsible for ocean freight, insurance, transshipment, destination port charges, and import customs.

FOB is transparent and fair. The supplier's price reflects costs they control and are experienced with — quarrying, inland transport, and Iranian export procedures — while the buyer controls the ocean freight, where they may have better rates through their own freight forwarder relationships. The proforma invoice should state "FOB Bandar Abbas" explicitly, along with the port charges included (typically CY charges, THC, and export documentation). When comparing FOB quotes from different suppliers, check whether port handling charges are included or billed separately, as this can vary by $150 to $350 per container.

## CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight): Supplier-Managed Shipping

Under CIF, the supplier adds ocean freight and marine insurance to the FOB cost and arranges shipping to the buyer's named destination port. The supplier books the vessel, pays the freight, and provides a minimum-level marine insurance policy (typically Institute Cargo Clauses C, covering major losses only). However, a critical and often misunderstood point is that risk still transfers to the buyer at the port of loading in Bandar Abbas — not at the destination port. This means if the container is damaged or lost at sea, the buyer bears the risk and must file the insurance claim, even though the supplier arranged the freight and policy.

CIF is convenient for buyers who prefer not to manage shipping logistics or who do not have established relationships with freight forwarders serving the Iran trade lane. The supplier handles everything up to the destination port, and the buyer only needs to arrange import customs and inland transport at destination. The trade-off is that CIF prices typically include a 5 to 15% markup on ocean freight and insurance compared to what an independent freight forwarder would charge. Buyers should always compare the supplier's CIF quote against a freight forwarder's quote for the same route to ensure the convenience premium is acceptable.

## Comparing Total Landed Cost Under Each Incoterm

The only way to truly compare quotes under different Incoterms is to calculate the total landed cost — the sum of all costs from quarry to your warehouse — under each option. For FOB, this means adding the FOB price, your freight forwarder's ocean freight quote, insurance (if you arrange it separately), destination THC, and import duties. For CIF, the supplier's price already includes freight and basic insurance, so you add only destination THC and import duties. For EXW, you must add inland trucking in Iran, export clearance fees, port charges, ocean freight, insurance, and destination charges.

A practical example: if a supplier quotes FOB Bandar Abbas at $280 per ton for travertine blocks, and your freight forwarder quotes $1,800 for a 20-foot container (28 tons) to your port, your ocean freight cost is approximately $64 per ton. If the same supplier offers CIF at $360 per ton, the implied freight and insurance is $80 per ton — a 25% markup over your forwarder's rate. In this case, FOB is clearly cheaper. But if you lack a freight forwarder and would need to hire one at a premium, the CIF convenience may be worth the difference. ## Risk Transfer Points and Insurance Considerations

Understanding exactly when risk transfers is critical for insuring your shipment correctly. Under EXW, risk is on the buyer from the moment the stone leaves the quarry — the buyer must insure the inland trucking, port handling, and ocean legs. Under FOB, risk transfers when the goods are loaded on the vessel at Bandar Abbas, so the buyer needs insurance covering the ocean transit and destination port handling. Under CIF, the supplier provides basic marine insurance (Clauses C), but this covers only total loss or major damage — it does not cover partial damage from shifting, water ingress, or container mishandling.

For high-value stone shipments, buyers under CIF should consider purchasing additional all-risks insurance (Institute Cargo Clauses A) to supplement the supplier's basic policy. The cost is typically 0.3 to 0.5% of the insured value. Buyers under FOB should arrange their own all-risks policy through their freight forwarder or insurance broker. Always confirm the insured value (usually 110% of the CIF value to cover incidental costs) and ensure the policy names you as the beneficiary so you can file claims directly.

## Choosing the Right Incoterm and Meeting Suppliers

For most buyers of Iranian stone, FOB Bandar Abbas is the recommended starting point. It balances the supplier's local expertise with the buyer's control over shipping costs, and it is the Incoterm most Iranian exporters are accustomed to quoting. CIF is a good option for buyers who value simplicity or are placing their first order and want the supplier to manage the full logistics chain. EXW should be reserved for experienced buyers with established Iran-based logistics operations.

Before committing to any Incoterm, meet with suppliers face to face to discuss terms, inspect quarry production, and build trust. ## How to Choose the Right Incoterm for Stone Export from Iran

### 1. Understand EXW (Ex Works)
Under EXW, the buyer is responsible for everything from the quarry — loading, inland transport, export clearance, and shipping. This gives maximum control but requires a logistics partner in Iran.

### 2. Understand FOB (Free on Board)
Under FOB Bandar Abbas, the supplier covers quarrying, inland transport to port, export clearance, and loading onto the vessel. Risk transfers to the buyer once the stone is on the ship.

### 3. Understand CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight)
Under CIF, the supplier adds ocean freight and insurance to the FOB cost. The supplier arranges shipping to your destination port, but risk still transfers at loading in Bandar Abbas.

### 4. Compare total costs
FOB is transparent — you control freight. CIF adds convenience but may include a markup on shipping. Compare your freight forwarder's quote against the supplier's CIF price.

### 5. Consider your logistics capabilities
If you have a reliable freight forwarder, FOB is usually more cost-effective. If you prefer the supplier to handle everything, CIF is simpler. EXW is only for buyers with a strong Iran-based logistics partner.

### 6. Confirm the Incoterm in the proforma invoice
Ensure the proforma invoice clearly states the Incoterm, the port (e.g., FOB Bandar Abbas), and what is included. This prevents disputes over who pays for what.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between FOB and CIF for Iranian stone exports?
FOB means the supplier covers costs up to loading on the vessel at Bandar Abbas; you arrange and pay for freight. CIF means the supplier also arranges and pays for ocean freight and insurance to your destination port.

### Which Incoterm is best for first-time buyers of Iranian stone?
FOB Bandar Abbas is recommended for most buyers. The supplier handles export complexity while you control shipping costs. If you prefer a simpler process, CIF lets the supplier arrange door-to-port delivery.

### What does EXW mean for stone purchases from Iran?
EXW (Ex Works) means you take responsibility for everything from the quarry — loading, transport to Bandar Abbas, export clearance, and shipping. It gives the lowest unit price but requires a logistics partner in Iran.

### When does risk transfer under FOB Bandar Abbas?
Under FOB, risk transfers from supplier to buyer once the goods are loaded onto the vessel at Bandar Abbas. From that point, you are responsible for loss or damage during transit.

### Is CIF more expensive than FOB for importing stone from Iran?
CIF includes the supplier's freight and insurance arrangements, which may carry a markup. If you have a good freight forwarder, FOB is often cheaper. Always compare the total landed cost under both terms.

### Can I switch from FOB to CIF after receiving a quote?
Yes. Most suppliers can provide both FOB and CIF quotes. If you start with FOB and later want the supplier to handle shipping, ask them to convert the quote to CIF with your destination port.
