# West Azerbaijan Stone: Quarry Sources and Export Logistics to Turkey and Iraq

West Azerbaijan is one of Iran's most strategically positioned stone-producing provinces. Located in the northwest, bordering Turkey and close to Iraq, it combines active granite, travertine, marble, and limestone quarries with short, efficient land routes to regional markets. For buyers in Turkey and Iraq, sourcing stone from West Azerbaijan can reduce both transit time and freight cost compared to quarries in central Iran or sea shipments via Bandar Abbas. This guide covers the province's quarry sources, stone types, pricing, and the land-export logistics that make West Azerbaijan a practical sourcing destination for regional stone buyers.

## Why West Azerbaijan Matters for Stone Export

Most Iranian stone exports move by sea from Bandar Abbas in the south. That works well for distant markets, but for Turkey and Iraq it adds thousands of kilometers of internal trucking before a container even reaches the port. West Azerbaijan flips that equation. Its quarries sit in the northwest, close to the Turkish border, so stone can move by road directly to the buyer's destination without touching a port.

The province's main quarrying hub is around **Urmia (Orumiyeh)**, the provincial capital. Urmia is roughly 200 km from the Bazargan border crossing into Turkey, making it one of the shortest quarry-to-border hauls in Iran. For Turkish buyers in particular, this geographic advantage is significant: a truck can leave a Urmia quarry and clear the Iranian side of the border within a single day.

West Azerbaijan also has a genuine diversity of stone. Unlike single-commodity regions, the province produces granite, travertine, marble, and limestone from different quarry belts. This lets regional buyers source multiple stone types from one province, consolidating shipments and simplifying supplier relationships.

## Quarry Sources Around Urmia

The quarries of West Azerbaijan cluster in the mountain belts surrounding Urmia and extend toward the border regions. Each belt tends to specialize, though there is overlap.

**Urmia granite quarries** produce predominantly gray and pink granite. The gray granite is a consistent, medium-grained stone used for construction, infrastructure, and monument bases. Pink granite serves decorative and cladding markets. Urmia granite is not as famous as Natanz red granite, but for buyers who need a reliable gray or pink building stone close to Turkey, it is one of the most cost-effective sources in Iran. The quarries are active year-round, and several have associated processing facilities for cutting blocks into slabs or tiles.

**Urmia travertine quarries** produce warm beige and light-cream travertine with the characteristic voids and layered texture that make travertine a favorite for building facades. West Azerbaijan travertine is used extensively in facade cladding across the region because it is light, easy to cut, and visually consistent in large volumes. The travertine from this province is particularly suitable for commercial building projects where large quantities of matching facade stone are needed.

**Marble and limestone** are also present in smaller quantities. West Azerbaijan marble tends toward cream and light-gray tones, used for interior flooring, wall cladding, and decorative work. Limestone from the province is used for both construction and architectural detailing. While marble and limestone are not the province's primary export commodities, they allow buyers to source mixed loads from a single region.

## Stone Types and Typical Applications

Understanding which stone suits which application helps buyers specify the right material from the start.

**Gray granite** from Urmia is the workhorse for construction. It is used for building bases, pavers, curb stones, and infrastructure projects. Its uniform gray color and high compressive strength make it suitable for both interior flooring and exterior load-bearing applications. For Turkish and Iraqi construction suppliers, gray granite is the highest-volume West Azerbaijan export.

**Pink granite** is used for decorative cladding, feature walls, and monument work. It is less common than gray but available in consistent quantities for projects that need a distinctive color.

**Travertine** is the leading facade stone. Its light weight, natural texture, and warm tones make it ideal for commercial building facades, especially large developments that need consistent appearance across thousands of square meters. West Azerbaijan travertine competes well with travertine from central Iran because the land route to Turkey and Iraq is so short.

**Marble and limestone** serve interior and architectural applications. Marble is used for flooring, wall cladding, and decorative surfaces. Limestone is used for architectural detailing and exterior stonework where a softer, warmer appearance is desired.

## FOB and DAP Pricing for West Azerbaijan Stone

Pricing for West Azerbaijan stone depends on the stone type, grade, block size, and delivery term. Because the province's main advantage is land export, DAP (Delivered at Place) border pricing is often more relevant than FOB port pricing for regional buyers.

**FOB Bandar Abbas** prices give a baseline for comparison. West Azerbaijan granite blocks typically range from $150 to $260 per ton, with gray granite at the lower end and pink granite higher. Travertine blocks range from $130 to $240 per ton depending on color uniformity and block size. Marble and limestone vary more widely depending on grade. These prices are competitive, but for Turkish and Iraqi buyers the FOB figure is only part of the delivered cost, because trucking from Urmia to Bandar Abbas (over 1,500 km) adds significantly to the total.

**DAP Bazargan border** pricing is where West Azerbaijan shines. Because the quarries are only about 200 km from the Bazargan crossing, the trucking component is small. DAP Bazargan pricing for granite typically runs $170 to $290 per ton, and for travertine $150 to $260 per ton, depending on grade and block size. For a Turkish buyer, comparing DAP Bazargan against FOB Bandar Abbas plus sea freight plus inland transport usually shows the land route as the clear winner on both cost and transit time.

**DAP Iraq borders** pricing depends on which crossing is used. For the Khosravi or Mehran borders, the haul from Urmia is much longer (over 1,000 km), so the trucking component is substantial. Buyers should compare DAP Iraq border pricing against the alternative of routing through Bazargan into Turkey and then onward to northern Iraq, which can sometimes be faster despite the double border crossing.

## Land Shipping Routes to Turkey and Iraq

The choice of route is the single biggest logistics decision for West Azerbaijan stone exports. Because the province is on the border, the options are clearer than for central-Iranian quarries.

### Land Route to Turkey via Bazargan

The Bazargan border crossing is the primary gateway from Iran to Turkey and sits inside West Azerbaijan province. The route from Urmia to Bazargan is roughly 200 km of mountain road, typically a half-day to full-day drive depending on load and weather. From Bazargan, trucks enter Turkey's eastern provinces and continue via the Turkish highway network to destinations across the country.

A truckload of stone blocks (typically 25–27 tons) can reach Istanbul from Bazargan in 3–4 days including border clearance. Eastern Turkish cities are reachable in 1–2 days. This is dramatically faster than sea freight, which requires trucking to Bandar Abbas, container loading, ocean transit to Mersin or Istanbul, and inland delivery.

For processed stone (slabs or tiles), the same route applies. Trucks are often curtain-sided or flatbed with A-frames for slabs, and the short border distance minimizes handling damage risk.

### Land Route to Iraq

For Iraq, West Azerbaijan stone can move two ways. The direct route trucks south and east to the **Khosravi (Khanaqin)** or **Mehran** border crossings, which connect to Iraq's central and southern provinces. This involves a long haul from Urmia (over 1,000 km) but is the standard route for most Iranian stone headed to Baghdad and southern Iraq.

The alternative for **northern Iraq (Kurdistan Region)** is to route through Bazargan into Turkey and then cross into the Kurdistan Region, or to use the **Parviz Khan** border crossing. For buyers in Erbil or Dohuk, the Bazargan-into-Turkey route can be competitive despite the extra border, because it avoids the long southward haul within Iran.

### Sea Route via Bandar Abbas

Sea freight remains an option for large-volume shipments, particularly for Iraqi buyers importing to **Umm Qasr port**. For West Azerbaijan stone, however, the sea route is usually less attractive than for central-Iranian quarries, because trucking the stone over 1,500 km south to Bandar Abbas erodes the geographic advantage. Sea freight becomes competitive only for very large volumes where container economies of scale outweigh the long pre-carriage haul.

## Export and Transit Documentation

Land exports from West Azerbaijan require a documentation set tailored to road transport. The core documents are the same as for any Iranian stone export, with one key addition.

**Standard export documents** include the commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and export declaration. The certificate of origin is issued by the Iran Chamber of Commerce and may require legalization by the Turkish or Iraqi embassy in Iran, depending on current requirements. Turkey may apply preferential tariffs based on the origin certificate, and Iraqi customs requires it for clearance.

**CMR consignment note** is the international road transport document required for land exports. It serves as the contract of carriage between the sender and the carrier and is mandatory for cross-border road transport under the CMR Convention. Your freight forwarder arranges the CMR.

**Transit documents** may be required if the truck crosses through Iran to reach a third country, or if the shipment transits Turkey on its way to a third destination. These allow the truck to pass through transit countries without being treated as an import. Your customs broker handles transit documentation where needed.

For a detailed breakdown of the standard document set, see our guide on [Iranian natural stone export documentation — bill of lading and packing list](/guides/iranian-natural-stone-export-documentation-bill-lading-packing-list/).

## Border Clearance Procedures

Border clearance is the most operationally complex part of a land export. At the Iranian exit point — Bazargan for Turkey, Khosravi or Mehran for Iraq — the truck presents the export declaration, CMR, and supporting documents to Iranian border customs. The cargo is verified and exit clearance is granted. On the destination side, the truck presents the same documents plus the certificate of origin to the importing country's customs, where import duties and taxes are assessed.

Having customs brokers on both sides is essential. The Iranian broker handles exit procedures; a Turkish or Iraqi broker handles entry. A good freight forwarder coordinates both, but you should confirm this is included in the service before booking. Delays at the border are most often caused by incomplete documents or missing broker coordination, not by the cargo itself.

## How to Export West Azerbaijan Stone to Turkey and Iraq

### 1. Identify the stone type from West Azerbaijan

West Azerbaijan produces granite, marble, travertine, and limestone. Identify which stone your Turkish or Iraqi project requires — gray granite for construction and infrastructure, travertine for facades, marble for interior cladding, limestone for architectural detailing. Sourcing multiple stone types from one province can consolidate your shipment.

### 2. Select quarries around Urmia

Most West Azerbaijan quarries cluster around Urmia and the surrounding mountains. Urmia granite and travertine quarries are the main export sources, with a short haul to the Bazargan border for Turkey. Confirm the quarry can supply the volume and grade you need, and request block or slab samples for approval.

### 3. Request FOB or DAP border pricing

For land exports, ask for DAP Bazargan (Turkey) or DAP Khosravi/Mehran (Iraq) pricing. FOB Bandar Abbas is available for sea shipments but the long internal haul makes it less competitive for regional buyers. Compare DAP border pricing against FOB plus sea freight plus inland transport to find the lowest delivered cost.

### 4. Choose the land shipping route

For Turkey, route via the Bazargan border crossing, roughly 200 km from Urmia. For Iraq, truck to the Khosravi or Mehran borders. Northern Iraq (Kurdistan Region) can also be reached via Bazargan through Turkey. Consider transit time, border conditions, and final destination when choosing between direct Iraq routes and transit-through-Turkey options.

### 5. Prepare export and transit documents

Prepare a commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, export declaration, and a CMR consignment note for road transport. Transit documents may be required if the truck passes through Iran or Turkey to a third destination. Ensure all document values, weights, and descriptions match exactly to avoid border delays.

### 6. Arrange border clearance on both sides

Use customs brokers at both the Iranian exit (Bazargan, Khosravi, or Mehran) and the destination entry point. Confirm your freight forwarder coordinates both sides so clearance proceeds without delay. Border delays are most often caused by incomplete documents or uncoordinated brokers, not by the cargo.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What types of stone does West Azerbaijan province produce for export?

West Azerbaijan produces granite (gray and pink), travertine, marble, and limestone. The quarries around Urmia are the main export sources. Granite and travertine are the most commonly exported varieties to Turkey and Iraq, with marble and limestone available in smaller quantities for interior and architectural applications.

### Why is West Azerbaijan a good sourcing region for Turkish buyers?

West Azerbaijan borders Turkey at the Bazargan crossing, only about 200 km from Urmia. This short haul makes land transport to eastern and central Turkey faster and cheaper than shipping from quarries in central Iran or via Bandar Abbas. A truck can reach Istanbul from Bazargan in 3–4 days including border clearance.

### What is the FOB price of West Azerbaijan granite and travertine blocks?

FOB Bandar Abbas prices for West Azerbaijan granite range from $150 to $260 per ton, while travertine ranges from $130 to $240 per ton depending on color uniformity and block size. DAP Bazargan border pricing is usually more competitive for Turkish buyers than FOB plus sea freight plus inland transport.

### Which border crossings are used for West Azerbaijan stone exports?

The Bazargan border is the main crossing to Turkey, located in West Azerbaijan itself. For Iraq, the Khosravi and Mehran borders are used, though these require a longer haul from Urmia. Northern Iraq can also be reached via Bazargan through Turkey, which is sometimes faster despite the extra border.

### What documents are needed to export stone from West Azerbaijan by road?

You need a commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, export declaration, and a CMR consignment note for road transport. Transit documents may be required for cross-border shipments through Iran or Turkey. The certificate of origin should be issued by the Iran Chamber of Commerce and legalized if required by the destination country.
