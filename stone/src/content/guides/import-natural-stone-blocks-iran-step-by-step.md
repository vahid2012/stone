# How to Import Natural Stone Blocks from Iran Step by Step

Importing natural stone blocks from Iran is a straightforward process when you work with a quarry-direct supplier who handles documentation and export logistics. Iran is one of the world's largest dimension stone producers, with extensive reserves of travertine, marble, granite, onyx, limestone, and crystal stone. For international buyers — whether stone factories, importers, or project contractors — sourcing directly from Iranian quarries offers significant cost advantages and access to premium stone varieties that are difficult to find elsewhere. This guide walks you through every step of the import process, from defining your requirements to receiving your shipment at your destination port.

## Why Iran Is a Leading Source for Natural Stone Blocks

Iran sits on one of the most geologically diverse stone reserves on the planet. The country has over 12,000 registered quarries spread across its provinces, producing travertine, marble, granite, onyx, limestone, and crystal stone in colors and patterns that rival the finest stones from Italy and Turkey. The travertine quarries around Azarshahr in East Azerbaijan and Takab in West Azerbaijan are among the most productive in the world. The Dehbid marble quarries in Fars province produce a creamy white marble that is highly sought after for large-format slabs and project work.

The cost advantage is significant. Iranian quarrying and labor costs are lower than those in Italy, Turkey, and most European countries, which translates into FOB prices that can be 30 to 50 percent below comparable European stone. For buyers importing blocks for resale or processing into slabs and tiles, this margin difference is substantial. Iran also produces unique varieties — such as Pietra Gray (a dark, fossil-bearing limestone from Harsin), red travertine from Azarshahr, and white onyx from central Iran — that have limited availability from other sources.

## Understanding the Iranian Stone Supply Chain

The export supply chain for Iranian stone blocks typically involves four stages. First, the quarry extracts raw blocks using diamond wire saws and chain saws. Second, a processing facility may trim or square the blocks to meet buyer specifications — standard quarry blocks range from 2 to 3 cubic meters, though oversized blocks of 5 cubic meters or more are available for certain stone types. Third, an export desk coordinates documentation, including the commercial invoice, certificate of origin, packing list, and export customs declarations. Fourth, the blocks are transported by truck to Bandar Abbas port on the Persian Gulf, containerized, and loaded onto vessels.

Bandar Abbas is Iran's primary stone export port. It handles the vast majority of containerized stone shipments and has regular sailings to major destinations including Jebel Ali (UAE), Mundra and Nhava Sheva (India), Chinese ports, and European ports via transshipment. Most quarry-direct exporters work with established freight forwarders at Bandar Abbas who manage container stuffing, vessel booking, and bill of lading issuance.

## Stone Types and Their Export Characteristics

Each stone type has distinct handling and export considerations. Travertine blocks from Azarshahr and Takab are the most commonly exported — they are dense, relatively lightweight compared to granite, and pack efficiently into containers. Silver and beige travertine dominate export volumes, while red and white travertine are premium varieties with higher per-ton prices.

Marble from Dehbid, Harsin, and Lashotor is another major export category. Dehbid white marble is particularly valued for its uniformity and large block sizes, making it ideal for book-matched slabs. Pietra Gray from Harsin is a consistent seller in Middle Eastern and Asian markets due to its dark tone and fossil patterns.

Granite from Natanz and Borujerd is exported in smaller volumes than travertine and marble but is growing in demand for infrastructure and cladding projects. Onyx — the rarest and most expensive export stone — comes from mines in central Iran and is typically shipped in smaller blocks or as slabs due to its high value and fragility.

## How to Import Natural Stone Blocks from Iran

The import process follows a clear sequence of steps. Below is a detailed walkthrough of each stage, from initial inquiry to final delivery at your destination port.

### 1. Identify your stone requirements

Define the stone type (travertine, marble, granite, onyx), block dimensions, volume in tons or cubic meters, and target delivery port. The more specific your spec, the more accurate your FOB quote. Include details like color preference (e.g., silver travertine vs. beige), grade (super, first, or commercial), and whether you need squared blocks or rough quarry blocks. If you are importing for slab processing, specify the minimum block size your gang saw or block cutter can handle — typically 280 cm or longer for large-format slabs.

### 2. Contact a quarry-direct supplier

A quarry-direct supplier can offer current quarry photos, block dimension lists, and same-week pricing. When you contact a supplier, ask about their quarry ownership or exclusive agreements — this tells you whether you are dealing directly with the source or through a trader who adds a margin. For guidance on evaluating suppliers, read our guide on [choosing the best Iranian marble and granite supplier](/guides/best-iranian-marble-granite-supplier-international-projects/).

### 3. Review the proforma invoice

The supplier sends a proforma invoice with stone type, block specs, quantity, unit price, total value, and payment terms. Review carefully and confirm before proceeding. Check that the HS code is correct (2515 for marble and travertine, 2516 for granite), that the currency is clearly stated (USD is standard for Iranian exports), and that the validity period gives you enough time to arrange payment and logistics. A typical proforma invoice is valid for 30 to 60 days.

### 4. Arrange payment

Iranian stone exports typically require payment via bank transfer or trade finance. Confirm the payment method and terms with your supplier and ensure compliance with your country's import regulations. Telegraphic transfer (T/T) is the most common method — typically 30 to 50 percent advance with the balance against scanned copies of shipping documents. Letters of credit (L/C) are accepted by some suppliers but may involve additional banking costs and longer processing times. Always verify that your payment route complies with applicable trade regulations regarding Iran in your jurisdiction.

### 5. Production and quality inspection

The supplier quarries and processes your blocks. Request photos and an inspection report before loading to verify dimensions, color consistency, and structural quality. A reliable supplier will provide photographs of each block from multiple angles, dimensional measurements, and notes on any veining, cracks, or color variation. For larger orders or first-time purchases, consider hiring a third-party inspection agency based in Iran — companies like SGS or local inspection firms can visit the processing yard and issue an independent quality report before loading.

### 6. Export documentation

The supplier prepares the certificate of origin, bill of lading, packing list, and commercial invoice. Review all documents for accuracy before the shipment departs. The certificate of origin is issued by the Iran Chamber of Commerce and confirms the stone was quarried in Iran — this is required by customs in most destination countries. The packing list should itemize each block with its dimensions and weight. For a comprehensive overview of documentation requirements, see our guide on [documents required for exporting marble from Iran](/guides/documents-exporting-marble-iran-certificate-origin/).

### 7. Loading and shipping FOB Bandar Abbas

Blocks are containerized and loaded at Bandar Abbas port. The supplier handles export customs clearance. You receive the bill of lading and track the shipment to your destination port. Standard 20-foot containers typically carry 25 to 28 tons of stone blocks, depending on the density of the material. Granite, being denser, may max out at 24 to 26 tons, while travertine can reach 27 to 28 tons. The supplier's export forwarder books the vessel, stuffs the container, and obtains the bill of lading from the shipping line within 2 to 5 days of loading.

### 8. Import customs clearance at destination

Upon arrival, use the shipping documents to clear import customs at your destination port. Pay applicable duties and arrange inland transport to your facility. You will need the original bill of lading, commercial invoice, certificate of origin, packing list, and any country-specific certificates (such as CE marking for the EU). Import duties for dimension stone vary widely — from 0 to 5 percent in some Gulf countries to 10 to 15 percent in certain Asian markets. Consult a local customs broker to confirm the duty rate and required certifications for your country.

## Choosing the Right Incoterm

The Incoterm you choose affects who is responsible for freight, insurance, and customs at each stage of the journey. FOB Bandar Abbas is the most common choice for first-time buyers because the supplier handles everything up to loading on the vessel — quarrying, processing, inland transport, export clearance, and container stuffing. You control the ocean freight and destination logistics, which gives you transparency over shipping costs.

For buyers who prefer a turnkey solution, CIF (Cost, Insurance, and Freight) or DAP (Delivered at Place) terms transfer freight arrangement to the supplier. This is convenient if you do not have an established freight forwarder, but it means the shipping cost is bundled into the price and is less transparent. To understand the differences in detail, see our guide on [Incoterms for stone export from Iran](/guides/incoterms-stone-export-iran-fob-cif-exw/).

## Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

Several issues commonly affect first-time importers of Iranian stone. The first is underestimating the importance of the proforma invoice — vague specs lead to blocks that do not match expectations. Always specify stone type, color, grade, minimum dimensions, and acceptable tolerance ranges.

The second pitfall is document discrepancies. A mismatch between the commercial invoice value and the certificate of origin, or a packing list that does not match the bill of lading, can cause days of delay at destination customs. Insist that your supplier send scanned copies of all documents for review before the vessel departs.

The third is overpaying for freight. If you choose FOB, shop around for freight forwarders with experience handling stone containers from Bandar Abbas. Stone is a heavy, low-value-per-weight commodity, so freight is a significant portion of the landed cost. For cost benchmarks, see our guide on [container shipping costs for natural stone from Iran](/guides/container-shipping-cost-natural-stone-iran-india-uae/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the minimum order quantity for natural stone blocks from Iran?

Most quarry-direct suppliers work with full container loads (approximately 25–28 tons per 20-foot container). Some suppliers accept trial orders of half containers for new buyers.

### How long does shipping from Bandar Abbas take?

Transit times vary by destination: 7–14 days to UAE and India, 18–25 days to China, 25–35 days to European ports. Your freight forwarder can provide an exact estimate.

### What documents do I need to import stone from Iran?

You need a commercial invoice, certificate of origin, bill of lading, packing list, and any country-specific certificates such as CE marking for the EU or radioactivity test reports.

### Can I inspect the blocks before shipping?

Yes. A reputable supplier provides photos, dimensions, and an inspection report before loading. You can also hire a third-party inspection agency in Iran for an independent check.

### What payment methods are accepted for Iranian stone exports?

Bank transfer (T/T) is most common. Some suppliers accept letters of credit. Payment terms and methods must comply with your country's trade regulations regarding Iran.

### Is FOB Bandar Abbas the best Incoterm for first-time buyers?

FOB is a good starting point because the supplier handles export clearance and loading. For door-to-door delivery, consider CIF or DAP, where the supplier arranges freight to your port.
