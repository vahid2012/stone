# European CE Marking and EN Standards for Importing Stone from Iran

CE marking is mandatory for many natural stone products sold in the European Union. If you are importing Iranian stone — travertine, marble, granite, or limestone — into the EU for construction use, understanding the CE marking process and applicable EN standards is essential. Without compliance, your products cannot be legally placed on the EU market. This guide explains the regulatory framework, the testing requirements, the certification process, and the responsibilities of both Iranian exporters and European importers in achieving and maintaining CE marking compliance.

## The Construction Products Regulation and Natural Stone

CE marking for natural stone is governed by the **Construction Products Regulation (CPR)** — Regulation (EU) No 305/2011. The CPR sets out the conditions for the marketing of construction products in the European Economic Area. Under the CPR, any construction product covered by a harmonized European standard must bear the CE mark when placed on the EU market.

Natural stone products covered by the CPR include slabs for cladding, paving slabs, facade slabs, tiles, and masonry units. The key principle is that the CE mark declares the product's performance in relation to its essential characteristics — it is not a quality mark, but a declaration that the product has been assessed against relevant European standards.

For Iranian stone exporters, this means that finished products such as travertine slabs, marble tiles, and granite paving must be CE marked before they can be sold to EU construction projects. Rough quarry blocks destined for further processing by a European factory are generally not covered by the CPR, since they are not yet construction products in their final form. However, if blocks are sold directly for use (e.g., for monument production), the requirements may differ.

## Applicable EN Standards for Natural Stone

The EN standard that applies depends on the product's intended end use. Each standard specifies the essential characteristics that must be declared and the test methods used to determine them.

- **EN 12058** — Slabs for cladding: Covers natural stone slabs used for wall cladding. Key characteristics include flexural strength, density, water absorption, and frost resistance.
- **EN 1341** — Slabs for external paving: Covers paving slabs used outdoors. Essential characteristics include flexural strength, abrasion resistance, slip resistance, and frost resistance.
- **EN 1469** — Slabs for facades: Covers stone slabs used on building facades, with emphasis on flexural strength and anchorage performance.
- **EN 12057** — Tiles: Covers natural stone tiles for flooring and wall covering. Characteristics include dimensional tolerances, flexural strength, and water absorption.
- **EN 771-6** — Natural stone masonry units: Covers stone blocks used in masonry construction.

For Iranian stone, the most relevant standards are typically EN 12058 and EN 1469 for travertine and marble cladding, and EN 1341 for granite paving. Travertine from Iran — particularly from the Azarshahr and Mahallat regions — is widely used in European cladding projects and frequently requires CE marking under EN 12058.

## Initial Type Testing (ITT)

Initial type testing is the first technical step in the CE marking process. ITT involves testing representative samples of the stone product to determine its performance against the essential characteristics specified in the applicable EN standard.

Testing must be performed by a **notified body** — an organization designated by an EU member state to carry out conformity assessment procedures. Notified bodies for natural stone testing include laboratories across Europe, such as CSTB (France), British Research Establishment (BRE, UK), and various accredited labs in Germany, Italy, and Spain.

The specific tests required depend on the EN standard but commonly include:

- **Flexural strength** (EN 12372): Measures the stone's resistance to bending — critical for cladding and paving.
- **Compressive strength** (EN 1926): Measures resistance to crushing loads.
- **Density** (EN 1936): Determines apparent density and open porosity.
- **Water absorption** (EN 13755): Measures water absorption at atmospheric pressure.
- **Abrasion resistance** (EN 14157): Measures wear resistance — important for flooring and paving.
- **Frost resistance** (EN 12371): Measures performance after freeze-thaw cycling — essential for outdoor applications.
- **Slip resistance** (EN 14231): Required for paving and flooring in public spaces.

Test samples must be representative of the product range. For a quarry producing multiple travertine varieties, each distinct type may require separate ITT. The results are documented in a test report that forms the basis for the Declaration of Performance.

## Factory Production Control (FPC)

Factory production control is the quality management system that the quarry or processing facility must implement and maintain. FPC ensures that the production consistently meets the performance levels declared in the DoP.

An FPC system includes documented procedures for:

- **Incoming material inspection**: Verifying the quality of blocks arriving from the quarry before processing.
- **Production monitoring**: Regular checks on cutting, calibrating, and finishing processes to ensure dimensional accuracy and surface quality.
- **Routine testing**: Periodic testing of key characteristics (e.g., flexural strength, water absorption) at defined intervals to verify consistency with ITT results.
- **Traceability**: A system to trace each batch of finished product back to the quarry source and production date.
- **Non-conformity management**: Procedures for handling products that fail to meet specifications.

The FPC system must be documented and available for inspection by the notified body or market surveillance authorities. For Iranian quarries and processors, implementing FPC may require developing new quality management procedures and training staff. Many larger Iranian processing facilities already have ISO 9001 systems in place, which provides a foundation for FPC compliance.

## The Declaration of Performance (DoP)

The Declaration of Performance is the key document in the CE marking framework. It is a formal statement by the manufacturer (or the importer, if the manufacturer is outside the EU) declaring the performance of the construction product in relation to its essential characteristics.

The DoP must include:

- The product type and its intended use
- The applicable harmonized standard (e.g., EN 12058)
- The manufacturer's name and address (or the importer's, if applicable)
- The system of assessment and verification of constancy of performance (AVCP)
- A table of declared essential characteristics with their performance values
- Reference to the test reports supporting the declared values

For natural stone, the AVCP system is typically **System 3** (initial type testing by a notified body, with FPC by the manufacturer) or **System 4** (manufacturer's own testing and FPC). System 3 is most common for stone products and requires notified body involvement for ITT.

The DoP must be available in the language of the EU member state where the product is placed on the market. The European importer typically handles translation and may take responsibility for issuing the DoP if the Iranian manufacturer has not done so.

## Affixing and Using the CE Mark

Once the DoP is issued, the CE mark must be affixed to the product, its packaging, or the accompanying commercial documents. For stone slabs and tiles, the CE mark is typically applied to the packaging (crates or bundles) and included on delivery documents rather than on each individual slab.

The CE mark must include:

- The **CE** symbol (in the prescribed format and minimum size)
- The **manufacturer's identification number** or name and registered address
- The **last two digits of the year** in which the mark was first affixed
- The **reference number** of the DoP
- The **notified body's identification number** (for System 3, the body that performed ITT)

Incorrectly affixed or incomplete CE marks are a common compliance issue. EU market surveillance authorities can request proof of CE marking at any time, and non-compliant products can be withdrawn from the market.

## Costs and Timeline

CE marking is an investment. The costs fall into two categories: initial certification and ongoing compliance.

**Initial type testing** costs depend on the number of tests required and the notified body's fees. For a single stone product type (e.g., travertine cladding slabs under EN 12058), ITT typically costs between €3,000 and €6,000. Each additional product type or standard requires separate testing.

**FPC implementation** costs vary widely. If the facility already has a quality management system, adapting it for FPC may cost a few thousand euros. Starting from scratch — including documentation, training, and internal audit — can cost €5,000–€10,000.

**Ongoing costs** include periodic retesting, FPC audits (if required by the notified body), and maintaining documentation. These are typically modest annual costs.

The timeline for initial CE marking is typically 4–8 weeks for ITT (depending on the notified body's workload and the number of tests), plus 2–4 weeks to implement FPC and prepare the DoP. If the facility already has quality systems in place, the overall timeline can be as short as 6 weeks.

## How to Obtain CE Marking for Iranian Natural Stone Exports to the EU

### 1. Identify the applicable EN standard

Natural stone products in the EU fall under EN 12058 (slabs for cladding), EN 1341 (slabs for external paving), EN 1469 (slabs for facades), and others depending on the product's intended use. Identify the end use of your product to determine the correct standard. If your product serves multiple end uses, multiple standards may apply.

### 2. Arrange initial type testing (ITT)

A notified body in Europe must perform initial type testing on samples of your stone — covering flexural strength, compressive strength, density, water absorption, abrasion resistance, and frost resistance. Select a notified body experienced with natural stone testing. The Iranian supplier provides representative samples from the relevant quarry.

### 3. Conduct factory production control (FPC)

The quarry or processing facility must implement a factory production control system with documented procedures for quality monitoring, testing frequency, and traceability. Document all procedures, train staff, and ensure records are maintained. If ISO 9001 is already in place, adapt existing procedures to meet FPC requirements.

### 4. Obtain the Declaration of Performance (DoP)

Based on ITT results and FPC, the supplier issues a Declaration of Performance stating the stone's essential characteristics and performance values for each declared property. The DoP must be translated into the language of the destination EU member state. The European importer may handle this step.

### 5. Affix the CE mark

The CE mark is affixed to the product, packaging, or accompanying documents. It must include the manufacturer's identification, the last two digits of the year of marking, and the DoP reference. For stone slabs, the mark is typically applied to crate labels and delivery documents. Ensure all required elements are present.

### 6. Maintain compliance records

Keep all test reports, FPC records, and DoPs on file. EU customs may request these during import clearance, and they must be available for market surveillance inspections. Records should be retained for at least 10 years after the last product was placed on the market, as required by the CPR.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do all Iranian stone products need CE marking to enter the EU?

CE marking is required for natural stone products covered by the Construction Products Regulation (CPR) — primarily slabs for cladding, paving, and flooring. Rough quarry blocks for further processing may not require CE marking, but finished products do. Check whether your specific product falls under a harmonized EN standard.

### What EN standards apply to Iranian natural stone?

Key EN standards include EN 12058 (slabs for cladding), EN 1341 (external paving slabs), EN 1469 (facade slabs), EN 12057 (tiles), and EN 771-6 (natural stone masonry units). The applicable standard depends on the product's end use. Multiple standards may apply if the product serves different applications.

### How much does CE marking certification cost for Iranian stone?

Costs vary depending on the stone type, number of tests required, and the notified body. Initial type testing typically costs several thousand euros, plus ongoing FPC audit costs. It is a one-time investment per product type. Budget €3,000–€6,000 for ITT and additional costs for FPC implementation.

### Can Iranian quarries obtain CE marking, or must it be done by a European importer?

Either the Iranian quarry/processor or the European importer can take responsibility for CE marking. In practice, the manufacturer (quarry or processor) typically performs ITT and FPC, while the importer may handle the Declaration of Performance. The arrangement should be agreed between supplier and importer before starting the process.

### What tests are required for CE marking of natural stone?

Common tests include flexural strength, compressive strength, density, water absorption, abrasion resistance, frost resistance, and for some products, slip resistance and thermal shock resistance. The exact set depends on the applicable EN standard and the product's intended use.

### How long does the CE marking process take for Iranian stone?

The process typically takes 4–8 weeks for initial type testing, plus time to implement factory production control. If the quarry already has quality systems in place, the timeline can be shorter. Plan for 8–12 weeks total from initial sampling to a completed DoP and affixed CE mark.
