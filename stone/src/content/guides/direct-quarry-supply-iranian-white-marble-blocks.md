# Direct Quarry Supply of Iranian White Marble Blocks for Stone Factories

For stone processing factories, the quality and consistency of raw block feedstock directly determines profitability. Iranian white marble — prized for its purity, workability, and aesthetic appeal — is a key input for factories producing slabs and tiles worldwide. Sourcing directly from quarries, rather than through traders, gives factories better pricing, consistent quality, and reliable supply. This guide explains how to identify the right white marble variety, specify block requirements, evaluate suppliers, and establish a direct quarry supply relationship for your factory.

## Iranian White Marble Varieties

Iran produces several distinct white marble varieties, each with unique characteristics that affect processing performance and end-product aesthetics. Understanding these differences is essential for selecting the right material for your factory's product line.

### Dehbid White Marble

Dehbid marble, quarried in Fars province in southern Iran, is considered Iran's finest white marble. It is characterized by a high-purity white background with subtle, light gray veining. The stone's compact crystalline structure gives it excellent polishability and a bright, luminous surface finish. Dehbid is favored for high-end architectural projects, luxury interior applications, and book-matched slab production.

Dehbid is classified into super, first, and commercial grades. Super grade Dehbid — with minimal veining and maximum whiteness — commands the highest prices and is often compared to premium white marbles from other countries. The quarry's production volume is moderate, which means large-volume contracts require advance planning.

### Harsin White Marble

Harsin marble, quarried in Kermanshah province in western Iran, is a creamy white marble with warmer tones than Dehbid. It may have light beige or gold veining and a slightly more pronounced pattern. Harsin is valued for its workability — it cuts cleanly, polishes well, and is less brittle than some higher-purity white marbles, making it suitable for tiles, mosaics, and cut-to-size products.

Harsin is generally more affordable than Dehbid, making it attractive for factories producing mid-range white marble products. The quarries in Kermanshah have good production capacity and can support consistent monthly volumes.

### Neyriz Crystal White Marble

Neyriz Crystal, also quarried in Fars province, is a white marble with a distinctive crystalline structure. It has a sparkle or crystalline texture visible on the polished surface, giving it a unique appearance that differentiates it from the more uniform Dehbid and Harsin. Crystal marble is popular for flooring, wall cladding, and decorative applications where a textured visual effect is desired.

The crystalline structure means Neyriz Crystal has slightly different processing characteristics — it may require different abrasive sequences for optimal polishing. Factories experienced with crystalline marbles can achieve excellent results.

## Block Grades and What They Mean

Iranian white marble blocks are graded based on visual quality, structural integrity, and consistency. The grade directly affects both price and the yield you can expect during processing.

**Super grade** blocks are the highest quality, with uniform white color, minimal veining, no visible cracks or fractures, and sound structure throughout. These blocks are suitable for book-matched slabs, high-end cladding, and applications where appearance is critical. Super grade blocks yield the highest percentage of premium slabs.

**First grade** blocks have minor color variation or light veining but are structurally sound. They may have small geological features that do not affect processing. First grade is the most common grade for standard slab production and offers a good balance of quality and cost.

**Commercial grade** blocks have more pronounced color variation, some veining, or minor structural features. They are suitable for tiles, cut-to-size products, and applications where the stone will be processed into smaller formats that can work around imperfections. Commercial grade offers the lowest cost per ton but also the lowest yield of premium slabs.

## Block Dimensions and Factory Requirements

Specifying the right block dimensions is critical for efficient factory operations. Blocks must fit your processing equipment and be large enough to produce slabs at economically viable sizes.

**Standard quarry blocks** in Iran are typically 2–3 cubic meters, measuring approximately 240×120×120 cm or similar. These blocks fit most gang saws and block cutters and are the default size from most quarries.

**Large blocks** up to 5–6 cubic meters (approximately 290×190×190 cm or larger) are available on request, particularly from Dehbid quarries. Large blocks are more efficient for gang saw processing because they produce more slabs per cutting cycle and yield larger slab formats. However, not all quarries can consistently produce large blocks, and they command a price premium.

**Small blocks** (under 2 cubic meters) are sometimes available at a discount but are less efficient for gang saws. They may be suitable for block cutters or for factories producing smaller format tiles.

When specifying dimensions, provide your supplier with:

- Maximum block dimensions your gang saw or cutter can accept (length, width, height)
- Minimum block dimensions that are economically viable for your equipment
- Preferred weight range (typically 20–30 tons for a standard 20-foot container)
- Any shape preferences (e.g., rectangular blocks rather than irregular shapes)

## FOB Bandar Abbas Pricing

Direct quarry supply eliminates the trader margins that apply when blocks pass through intermediaries. FOB Bandar Abbas pricing for Iranian white marble blocks ranges from $250 to $500 per ton, depending on the variety, grade, and block size.

**Dehbid white marble**:
- Super grade: $400–$500 per ton
- First grade: $300–$400 per ton
- Commercial grade: $250–$300 per ton

**Harsin white marble**:
- Super grade: $300–$380 per ton
- First grade: $250–$320 per ton
- Commercial grade: $200–$250 per ton

**Neyriz Crystal white marble**:
- First grade: $280–$360 per ton
- Commercial grade: $220–$280 per ton

Large blocks (over 4 cubic meters) typically carry a 10–20% premium over standard blocks due to their scarcity and higher yield potential. Prices are quoted per ton, and a standard 20-foot container holds 25–28 tons of marble blocks.

Pricing is influenced by market demand, quarry production levels, and seasonal factors. Demand for Dehbid super grade is consistently high, and prices can firm during peak construction seasons. Negotiating a fixed-price supply agreement can protect your factory from price fluctuations.

## Evaluating Block Yield

Block yield — the ratio of usable finished slabs to raw block weight — is one of the most important factors in determining your actual cost per square meter of finished product. A lower-priced block with poor yield can end up costing more per square meter than a higher-priced block with excellent yield.

Factors affecting yield include:

- **Block integrity**: Cracks, fractures, and voids reduce yield. Super grade blocks have the highest yield.
- **Block shape**: Rectangular blocks yield more than irregular shapes, as there is less waste during squaring.
- **Veining patterns**: Heavy or irregular veining may require cutting around defects, reducing usable slab area.
- **Hardness and cutting behavior**: Softer marbles cut faster but may have more breakage; harder marbles take longer but can produce more intact slabs.

Ask your supplier about typical yield for the specific quarry and grade. A reputable quarry-direct supplier should be able to provide yield data from previous shipments. For a new supplier, a trial container is the best way to verify yield under your factory's actual processing conditions.

## How to Source Direct Quarry Supply of Iranian White Marble Blocks

### 1. Identify the white marble variety

Iran produces several white marbles: Dehbid (high-purity white with subtle veins), Harsin (creamy white), and Crystal (Neyriz, with crystalline structure). Each has different processing characteristics. Match the variety to your factory's product line — Dehbid for premium slabs, Harsin for mid-range tiles, and Neyriz Crystal for decorative applications.

### 2. Specify block grade and dimensions

Factories need blocks that fit their gang saw or block cutter. Specify maximum and minimum block dimensions, grade (super, first, commercial), and required yield ratio. Provide your equipment's cutting dimensions and preferred weight range. Clear specifications prevent receiving blocks that do not fit your production line.

### 3. Request quarry-direct FOB pricing

Direct quarry pricing eliminates trader margins. Request FOB Bandar Abbas per-ton pricing for your specified grade and dimensions. White marble blocks typically range from $250 to $500 per ton. Compare pricing across multiple quarries for the same grade and variety to ensure competitive rates.

### 4. Evaluate block yield

Ask the supplier about typical block yield — the ratio of usable slabs to raw block weight. High-yield blocks reduce your cost per square meter of finished slabs. Request yield data from previous shipments and consider how yield affects your true cost per square meter, not just the per-ton price.

### 5. Plan a trial container

For a new quarry source, order one trial container (25–28 tons) to evaluate block quality, yield, and processing performance before committing to regular supply. Process the trial blocks through your standard production line and measure yield, slab quality, and any processing issues. This small investment prevents costly mistakes with larger orders.

### 6. Establish a supply agreement

Once quality is confirmed, negotiate a supply agreement with fixed pricing, monthly volume, block specifications, and delivery schedule for consistent factory feedstock. A formal agreement protects both parties and ensures your factory has reliable input material. Include specifications for grade, dimensions, yield expectations, and pricing terms.
