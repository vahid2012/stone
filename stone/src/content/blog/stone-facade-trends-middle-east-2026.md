# Stone Facade Trends in the Middle East 2026: What Architects Are Specifying

The Middle East has always been a market where natural stone facades signal quality, permanence, and architectural ambition. From the travertine-clad towers of Doha to the marble facades of Riyadh, stone remains the preferred exterior material for landmark commercial and residential projects. As we look at 2026, several trends are shaping what architects specify.

## Trend 1: Large-Format Travertine Panels

Iranian travertine — particularly beige and silver from Azarshahr — is being specified in increasingly large formats. Panels of 120x60cm and larger create a seamless, contemporary look that reduces grout lines and emphasizes the stone's natural texture.

## Trend 2: Honed and Brushed Finishes

While polished stone remains popular for interiors, exterior facades are shifting toward honed and brushed finishes. These finishes reduce glare in intense sunlight, hide dust and weathering better than polished surfaces, and provide a sophisticated, matte appearance.

## Trend 3: Mixed Stone Tones

Architects are combining multiple stone tones — warm beige travertine with cool gray marble accents, or white limestone with dark Pietra Gray features — to create visual depth and contrast on large facades.

## Trend 4: Sustainable Sourcing

With growing awareness of embodied carbon, architects are increasingly asking about quarry practices, transport distances, and stone durability. Iranian stone's natural durability (100+ year lifespan) and relatively short shipping distance to Gulf markets make it a sustainable choice.

## Trend 5: Rainscreen Systems

Modern stone facades increasingly use rainscreen cladding systems, where stone panels are mounted on an aluminum subframe with an air gap. This improves thermal performance and allows for natural ventilation.
