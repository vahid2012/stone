# Iranian Travertine and Marble Export FOB Price per Ton at Bandar Abbas

Understanding FOB pricing for Iranian travertine and marble is essential for any stone buyer planning to import from Iran. FOB (Free on Board) at Bandar Abbas means the supplier covers all costs up to loading your stone onto the vessel — quarrying, processing, inland transport to the port, export documentation, and customs clearance. You, as the buyer, are responsible for ocean freight, insurance, and import clearance at your destination. This guide breaks down current FOB price ranges per ton for the most popular Iranian travertine and marble varieties, explains the factors that drive pricing, and shows you how to request an accurate, binding quote from a quarry-direct supplier.

## What FOB Bandar Abbas Actually Includes

When a supplier quotes you an FOB Bandar Abbas price, that figure is not just the cost of the stone at the quarry. It is a bundled price that includes several cost layers. First, the quarrying cost — extracting the block from the mine face using diamond wire saws or chain saws. Second, the processing cost — squaring, trimming, and sometimes resin-filling the block to meet buyer specifications. Third, inland transport from the quarry to Bandar Abbas port, which can range from 800 to 1,500 kilometers depending on the quarry location. Quarries in Fars province (Dehbid) and East Azerbaijan (Azarshahr) are among the farthest from the port, adding meaningfully to the FOB cost. Fourth, export documentation and customs clearance fees, including the certificate of origin from the Iran Chamber of Commerce. Fifth, port handling and container stuffing at Bandar Abbas.

Understanding this breakdown matters because it explains why FOB prices for the same stone type can vary between suppliers. A supplier with a quarry close to Bandar Abbas has lower inland transport costs and can offer a more competitive FOB price. A supplier whose quarry is in northwestern Iran faces higher trucking costs, which are built into the FOB figure. When comparing quotes, always ask which quarry the stone comes from — this gives you insight into the transport cost component.

## Travertine FOB Price Ranges by Color and Grade

Travertine is Iran's flagship export stone, and pricing varies significantly by color, grade, and origin. Silver travertine from Azarshahr is the most commonly exported variety. FOB Bandar Abbas prices for silver travertine blocks typically range from $150 to $200 per ton for commercial grade, $180 to $230 for first grade, and $220 to $280 for super grade. The super grade is characterized by tight pore structure, consistent silver-gray coloration, and minimal veining — suitable for high-end slab processing.

Beige travertine, also primarily from Azarshahr and Takab, is priced similarly to silver — $160 to $210 per ton for commercial and first grades, with super grade reaching $240 to $280. Beige travertine is the most versatile variety and sees strong demand from buyers in China, India, and the Gulf countries.

Red travertine is a premium variety produced in limited quantities from select quarries in Azarshahr. FOB prices for red travertine blocks range from $250 to $350 per ton, reflecting its scarcity and high demand from luxury architectural projects. White travertine, the rarest color category, commands $280 to $400 per ton when available in block sizes suitable for slab cutting.

## Marble FOB Price Ranges by Type

Iranian marble is more varied in pricing than travertine because the stone types span a wider quality and aesthetic range. Pietra Gray (also known as Anarak limestone) from Harsin in Kermanshah province is a consistent mid-range export stone. FOB Bandar Abbas prices for Pietra Gray blocks typically range from $200 to $280 per ton, depending on block size and the density of the characteristic white fossil veining. Pietra Gray is one of the most popular Iranian marbles in Middle Eastern and South Asian markets.

Harsin cream marble, also from Kermanshah, is priced at $220 to $300 per ton FOB. It is a warm, beige-toned marble used extensively for flooring and wall cladding. Lashotor marble from Isfahan province, a light cream marble with subtle veining, falls in a similar range.

Dehbid white marble from Fars province is Iran's premium white marble. It is prized for its brightness, uniformity, and ability to yield large book-matched slabs. FOB prices for Dehbid white marble blocks range from $350 to $500 per ton, with super-grade blocks exceeding $500 for exceptional material. The high price reflects the distance of Dehbid quarries from Bandar Abbas (approximately 1,200 kilometers), the large block sizes available, and strong demand from European and Chinese buyers.

White crystal marble (sometimes called crystal stone) from quarries in central Iran is another premium category. FOB prices range from $300 to $450 per ton. Crystal marble is distinguished by its glassy, translucent quality and is used for high-end interior applications.

## How to Get an Accurate FOB Price for Iranian Travertine and Marble

Getting an accurate, binding FOB price requires providing your supplier with precise specifications. Vague requests like "send me your travertine price" produce vague quotes that are unlikely to hold when it is time to ship. Below is a step-by-step process to get a quote you can rely on.

### 1. Specify stone type and grade

Indicate whether you need travertine (silver, beige, red, white) or marble (Pietra Gray, Harsin, Dehbid, White Crystal) and the quality grade — super, first, or commercial. The grade determines not only the price but the suitability of the stone for your application. Super grade is essential for book-matched slabs and high-end projects; commercial grade is adequate for cut-to-size tiles and projects where color variation is acceptable.

### 2. Define block dimensions

Block size affects price per ton. Standard quarry blocks (2–3 cubic meters) are more economical than oversized or custom-cut blocks. If your gang saw requires blocks longer than 280 cm, specify this — oversized blocks carry a premium because fewer quarries can produce them. Conversely, if you accept smaller blocks (1.5–2 cubic meters), some suppliers can offer a lower per-ton price since smaller blocks are easier to produce and handle.

### 3. State your volume

Volume impacts pricing — full container loads (25–28 tons) get better per-ton rates than partial loads. Project volumes (multiple containers) may qualify for additional discounts. If you are buying 5 containers or more for a project, mention this in your initial inquiry — quarry-direct suppliers often have volume tiers that reduce the per-ton price by 5 to 15 percent.

### 4. Confirm Incoterm

FOB Bandar Abbas means the supplier covers costs up to loading on the vessel. CIF adds freight and insurance. Confirm which Incoterm your quote is based on. This is critical because a CIF quote that looks attractive may include a freight markup, while an FOB quote gives you control over shipping costs. If you are comparing suppliers, ensure all quotes are on the same Incoterm basis — comparing an FOB quote from one supplier with a CIF quote from another leads to apples-to-oranges confusion. For a deeper comparison of Incoterms, see our guide on [Incoterms for stone export from Iran](/guides/incoterms-stone-export-iran-fob-cif-exw/).

### 5. Request a proforma invoice

Ask the supplier for a proforma invoice with unit price per ton, total value, currency (USD), and validity period. This locks in the price for a set timeframe. A proforma invoice is more than a price list — it is a commercial commitment. It should include the HS code (2515 for marble and travertine), the stone type and grade, the block quantity and estimated total weight, the port of loading (Bandar Abbas), and the payment terms. A valid proforma invoice gives you 30 to 60 days to arrange payment and logistics before the price is subject to revision.

### 6. Compare with market benchmarks

Cross-reference the quote against current market indicators and quotes from other suppliers. Remember that quarry-direct pricing should be significantly lower than trader pricing. If a quote is 20 percent below the market range, investigate — it may indicate commercial-grade material being offered as first grade, or a supplier without the actual quarry capacity to deliver. If a quote is 20 percent above the market range, ask the supplier to justify the premium. ## Factors That Drive Price Variation

Beyond stone type and grade, several factors influence FOB pricing. Quarry location is a major one — as noted, the inland transport cost from quarry to Bandar Abbas can vary by hundreds of dollars per container. A travertine block from Azarshahr (approximately 1,500 km to Bandar Abbas) carries higher trucking costs than a block from a quarry in Hormozgan province near the port.

Seasonal demand also affects pricing. The Chinese market, Iran's largest stone export destination, tends to place heavy orders ahead of the spring construction season, which can push prices up in January through March. Conversely, demand softens during the summer months in the Gulf region, which can create buying opportunities.

Currency fluctuations play a role as well. Iranian stone is priced in USD for export, but quarrying and processing costs are incurred in Iranian rials. When the rial depreciates against the dollar, suppliers have room to offer more competitive USD prices. Conversely, a strengthening rial can put upward pressure on FOB quotes.

Block quality consistency is another factor. A supplier who can guarantee uniform color and texture across a multi-container order commands a premium because that consistency saves the buyer time and waste during processing. ## FOB vs. CIF: Which Is Better for You?

The FOB vs. CIF decision comes down to who controls the freight and how much transparency you want in pricing. Under FOB Bandar Abbas, you arrange and pay for ocean freight directly. This means you see the freight cost as a separate line item and can shop around for the best rate. If you have a relationship with a freight forwarder who handles Middle East routes, FOB is almost always the cheaper option.

Under CIF, the supplier arranges freight and insurance and bundles those costs into a single price. This is convenient if you do not have a freight forwarder or if you want a single point of contact for the entire shipment. However, the supplier typically adds a margin to the freight cost, and you have less visibility into the actual shipping expense. For first-time buyers without logistics experience, CIF can be a reasonable choice. For experienced importers, FOB is usually more cost-effective.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the FOB price of Iranian travertine per ton at Bandar Abbas?

FOB prices for Iranian travertine blocks typically range from $150 to $280 per ton depending on color, grade, and block size. Silver and beige travertine are at the lower end; red and white travertine command higher prices.

### How much does Iranian marble cost per ton FOB Bandar Abbas?

Iranian marble blocks range from $200 to $500 per ton FOB. Pietra Gray and Harsin marble are mid-range; Dehbid white marble and high-grade crystal marble are at the upper end.

### What factors affect the FOB price of Iranian stone?

Stone type, color, grade, block dimensions, volume, quarry accessibility, and market demand all influence FOB pricing. Seasonal factors and currency fluctuations also play a role.

### Is FOB Bandar Abbas cheaper than CIF for my destination?

FOB is transparent — you control freight costs. If you have a good freight forwarder, FOB is often cheaper than CIF. If you prefer the supplier to handle shipping, CIF adds convenience at a premium.

### How often do Iranian stone FOB prices change?

Prices can fluctuate quarterly based on quarry output, domestic demand, and currency exchange rates. Proforma invoices typically lock in a price for 30–60 days.

### Can I negotiate the FOB price with a quarry-direct supplier?

Yes. Quarry-direct suppliers like StoneBuySell have more flexibility than traders. Larger volumes, repeat orders, and flexible delivery timelines can all improve your negotiated price.

### Are there additional costs beyond the FOB price?

Yes. Beyond FOB, you pay ocean freight, insurance (if CIF), import duties, and inland transport at your destination. Export documentation and loading are included in FOB.
