# Documents Required for Exporting Marble from Iran: Certificate of Origin and More

Exporting marble from Iran requires a specific set of documents to ensure smooth customs clearance at both the origin and destination. The certificate of origin is the most critical — it proves the marble was quarried in Iran and is required by customs authorities worldwide. But it is not the only document you need. This guide covers every document required for a marble export from Iran, from the commercial invoice to the bill of lading, packing list, and special certificates like CE marking and radioactivity test reports. Whether you are a first-time buyer or an experienced importer, understanding the documentation package is essential to avoid delays, demurrage charges, and clearance problems at your destination port.

## The Core Export Document Package

Every marble export from Iran involves a core set of documents that must travel with — or ahead of — the shipment. These documents serve distinct legal and commercial functions, and customs authorities at the destination port will require all of them before releasing the cargo. The core package consists of the commercial invoice, certificate of origin, packing list, and bill of lading. In addition, depending on the destination country, you may need special certificates such as CE marking for the European Union, radioactivity test reports, or a quality inspection report.

The key principle is consistency. Every document must tell the same story — the same buyer and seller names, the same stone description, the same HS code, the same quantities, and the same total value. A discrepancy of even a single digit between the commercial invoice and the certificate of origin can trigger a customs hold that costs you days of demurrage. For this reason, a well-organized supplier sends scanned copies of all documents to the buyer for review before the vessel departs Bandar Abbas.

## The Commercial Invoice

The commercial invoice is the foundation document of the entire export transaction. It is prepared by the supplier and serves as the primary record of the sale. A properly prepared commercial invoice for Iranian marble exports includes the following elements: full name and address of the seller (the Iranian exporter) and the buyer (the importing party); a detailed description of the goods including stone type, color, grade, and form (blocks, slabs, or tiles); the HS code (2515 for marble, travertine, and alabaster; 2516 for granite); the unit price per ton or per square meter; the total quantity and total invoice value; the currency (USD is standard for Iranian stone exports); the country of origin (Iran); the port of loading (Bandar Abbas); the payment terms (e.g., T/T 30% advance, 70% against documents); and the invoice date and reference number.

The commercial invoice must be printed on the supplier's company letterhead, stamped, and signed. Some destination countries require the invoice to be legalized by their embassy in Iran or certified by the Iran Chamber of Commerce. Your customs broker at the destination can advise on whether legalization is required for your country.

## The Certificate of Origin

The certificate of origin (COO) is the document that certifies the marble was quarried and processed in Iran. It is issued by the Iran Chamber of Commerce, Industries, Mines and Agriculture (ICCIMA) and is one of the most important documents in the export package. Customs authorities in virtually all destination countries require a certificate of origin to verify that the goods originated from the declared country and to determine applicable tariff rates.

The COO includes the exporter's name and address, the importer's name and address, a description of the goods, the HS code, the gross and net weight, the number of packages, and a declaration that the goods are of Iranian origin. It is stamped and signed by the Chamber of Commerce and, in some cases, further legalized by the destination country's embassy or consulate in Iran.

For buyers in countries that have preferential trade arrangements with Iran, the COO can reduce or eliminate import duties. Even where no preferential arrangement exists, the COO is mandatory — without it, customs will not clear the shipment. The certificate of origin typically takes 2 to 3 days to issue from the time the supplier submits the application to the Chamber of Commerce. ## The Packing List

The packing list provides a detailed itemization of the shipment. For marble blocks, the packing list should include: the container number and seal number; the total number of blocks or packages; for each block — the dimensions (length × width × height in centimeters), the weight in kilograms, and the block identification number; the total gross weight and net weight of the container; and the stone type and grade.

Customs authorities use the packing list to verify that the physical shipment matches what is declared on the commercial invoice and certificate of origin. If the packing list shows 12 blocks totaling 27.5 tons but the commercial invoice declares 28 tons, customs may flag the discrepancy. The packing list is also used by the buyer's warehouse team to plan unloading and storage, and by the processing facility to plan block cutting.

A best practice is to have the packing list prepared at the time of container stuffing at Bandar Abbas, with the actual block dimensions and weights recorded as each block is loaded. This ensures the document reflects the real contents of the container, not an estimate.

## The Bill of Lading

The bill of lading (B/L) is issued by the shipping line or the freight forwarder once the container is loaded onto the vessel at Bandar Abbas. It is a three-function document: it serves as a receipt for the goods, a contract of carriage between the shipper and the carrier, and a document of title that can be used to transfer ownership of the goods while they are in transit.

The B/L includes the shipper's name (the Iranian supplier), the consignee's name (the buyer or their bank, in the case of L/C transactions), the notify party, the vessel name and voyage number, the port of loading (Bandar Abbas), the port of discharge (the destination port), the container number and seal number, a description of the goods, the gross weight, and the number of packages.

There are two main types of bill of lading for stone shipments: original B/L and sea waybill (express release). An original B/L is a negotiable document — the buyer needs the physical original copies to claim the goods at the destination. The supplier typically sends the original B/L by courier (DHL, FedEx) to the buyer after the vessel departs. A sea waybill is non-negotiable and allows the consignee to claim the goods without presenting the original document — this is faster and is used when the supplier and buyer have an established relationship and payment has been settled.

The B/L is typically issued within 2 to 5 days after loading. For a detailed breakdown of the full documentation workflow, see our guide on [Iranian natural stone export documentation](/guides/iranian-natural-stone-export-documentation-bill-lading-packing-list/).

## How to Prepare Export Documents for Iranian Marble

The document preparation process follows a clear sequence. Below is a step-by-step walkthrough of how each document is obtained and what to check at each stage.

### 1. Obtain a commercial invoice

The supplier prepares a commercial invoice with buyer/seller details, stone description, HS code, unit price, total value, and payment terms. This is the foundation document for all exports. Ensure the buyer's name and address match exactly what will appear on the import declaration at the destination — any mismatch can cause clearance delays.

### 2. Secure a certificate of origin

Issued by the Iran Chamber of Commerce, the certificate of origin confirms the marble was quarried in Iran. This is required by customs in most destination countries and may qualify you for preferential tariffs. The supplier submits an application to the Chamber of Commerce along with the commercial invoice and quarry documentation. Processing takes 2 to 3 days. If your country requires embassy legalization, add another 3 to 7 days to the timeline.

### 3. Prepare the packing list

The packing list details each block or slab — dimensions, weight, quantity, and container number. Customs authorities use this to verify the shipment matches the invoice. The packing list should be finalized at the time of container stuffing, with actual measurements recorded as blocks are loaded. Insist that your supplier provide a photo of each block alongside its recorded dimensions for cross-referencing.

### 4. Issue the bill of lading

The shipping line issues the bill of lading once the container is loaded at Bandar Abbas. This document serves as a receipt, a contract of carriage, and proof of title to the goods. Confirm with the supplier whether the B/L will be an original or a sea waybill, and ensure the consignee details are correct — changing the consignee after issuance is a time-consuming and costly process.

### 5. Obtain special certificates if required

Depending on your destination, you may need a CE marking certificate (EU), a radioactivity test report (EU), a phytosanitary certificate, or a quality inspection report. Plan for these in advance — CE marking and radioactivity testing can take 1 to 2 weeks and must be completed before the shipment departs. Your customs broker at the destination can provide a definitive list of required certificates for your country.

### 6. Verify all documents for consistency

Before the vessel departs, cross-check that names, values, HS codes, and quantities match across all documents. Discrepancies cause delays at destination customs. Create a simple checklist: compare the buyer name, seller name, stone description, HS code, total weight, total value, and container number across the commercial invoice, COO, packing list, and B/L. If any field does not match, request a correction before the vessel sails.

## Special Certificates for EU and Other Regulated Markets

For buyers importing Iranian marble into the European Union, additional certificates are required. The CE marking, conforming to EN 12057 (for modular tiles) and EN 1469 (for slabs), certifies that the stone meets EU standards for mechanical properties, slip resistance, and radioactivity. The radioactivity test report is particularly important — it measures the concentration of naturally occurring radioactive elements (radium-226, thorium-232, and potassium-40) in the stone and confirms that it falls below the EU's acceptable thresholds. Most Iranian marbles and travertines pass these tests without issue, but the testing must be conducted by an accredited laboratory and the certificate must accompany the shipment.

For other markets, requirements vary. Some Gulf countries require a certificate of conformity from an approved inspection agency. Certain Asian markets require a pre-shipment inspection (PSI) certificate. Your freight forwarder or customs broker can advise on the specific requirements for your destination.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a certificate of origin and why is it needed for marble exports from Iran?

A certificate of origin is an official document certifying that the marble was quarried in Iran. It is issued by the Iran Chamber of Commerce and is required by customs in most countries to verify the country of origin.

### Who issues the bill of lading for marble exports from Bandar Abbas?

The shipping line or freight forwarder issues the bill of lading once the container is loaded onto the vessel at Bandar Abbas. It serves as a receipt, contract of carriage, and document of title.

### Do I need a CE marking to export marble from Iran to the EU?

Yes, for certain marble products entering the EU, CE marking per EN standards is required. This involves testing for mechanical properties, slip resistance, and radioactivity.

### What HS code is used for Iranian marble exports?

Marble blocks and slabs typically fall under HS code 2515 (marble, travertine, and alabaster). Your supplier should confirm the exact sub-code based on the product form.

### How long does it take to prepare all export documents for marble from Iran?

A well-organized supplier can prepare all documents within 3–7 days. The certificate of origin from the Chamber of Commerce typically takes 2–3 days, while the bill of lading is issued at loading.

### Can I get a quality inspection report for my marble shipment?

Yes. Reputable suppliers provide photos and inspection reports before loading. You can also hire a third-party inspection agency in Iran for an independent quality check.
